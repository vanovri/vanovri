These 10 demonstrations from Google Cloud Tech’s showcase (*"Gemini 4 Argon: 10 incredible AI demos"*, presented by Smitha Kolan and Shubham Saboo) illustrate what changes when a frontier model combines a **1-million-token combined input/output window** with long-horizon agentic execution in **Google Antigravity** and **Google AI Studio**. Rather than generating isolated code snippets, **Gemini 4 Argon** (released in October 2026 at $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens) sustains multi-hour tool-calling, compiler feedback, and visual self-correction loops across entire repositories and 3D pipelines.

## How the 10 Demos Break Down by Engineering Pattern

### 1. Autonomous Codebase Refactoring & Self-Healing Loops (Demos 1–3)

* **1960s Fortran Migration (01:26):** Demonstrates cross-era architectural translation—reading monolithic legacy scientific Fortran and outputting idiomatic, memory-safe **Rust** and modern **C++** in a single long-horizon pass while maintaining numerical parity.
* **ADK Python Optimization (02:17):** Based on an 11-hour unattended overnight run across Google’s open-source Agent Development Kit (`google/adk-python`), which spans roughly **205,000 lines of code** across nearly **800 files** and **17,000 unit tests**. By holding cross-file execution paths in context, Argon cut in-memory session preparation overhead by **40%** (measured at 50 and 150 turns) and accelerated cold package imports by **21%** without breaking the test suite.
* **Autonomous Android UI (03:02):** Converts a flat 2D map into a 3D spatial Android interface while using an automated build-and-fix loop—reading Gradle/compiler errors, patching dependencies, and re-running builds autonomously until compilation succeeds.

### 2. Multimodal-to-3D & Browser Graphics Pipelines (Demos 4–6)

* **Desk Racer (04:20):** Takes everyday phone photos of a physical desk, maps real-world obstacles (monitor, keyboard, mug, books), generates 2D concept mocks and textures via **Nano Banana**, models 3D assets through **Blender MCP**, and wires up a playable **Three.js** browser racing game complete with collision meshes, lap timers, and chase cameras.
* **Pagoda 3D Model (05:12):** Highlights multi-agent orchestration and **Adversarial Critic subagents**. Instead of stopping after a rough first draft, Argon spawns a separate critic agent that inspects rendered screenshots from multiple camera angles, scores geometry and lighting, and forces iterative refinements in headless Blender and Three.js until the model clears a high quality threshold.
* **WebGPU Fur Simulation (06:07):** Generates low-level compute and render shaders from scratch to simulate thousands of interactive hair and fur strands in real time inside the browser, including device capability checks and GPU fallbacks.

### 3. Scientific Telemetry & Medical Digital Twins (Demos 7–8)

* **3D DEXA Body Twin (07:35):** Bridges unstructured document extraction and spatial coding by parsing flat medical body-composition scan reports and synthesizing an interactive 3D anatomical twin for visualizing bone density, lean mass, and fat distribution.
* **NASA Artemis II Visualization (08:35):** Ingests raw **NASA Artemis II** flight-derived ephemeris telemetry (CCSDS OEM trajectory data from the April 2026 lunar flyby mission) and writes the orbital mechanics math and 3D rendering code to plot the spacecraft's real-time path around the Moon.

### 4. Physical Hardware & Sensor-Driven Experiences (Demos 9–10)

* **Agent Gadgets (09:56):** Moves from rough hand-drawn napkin sketches to parametric, 3D-printable hardware enclosures (`STL`/`STEP`), accounting for physical component tolerances and casing fit.
* **Argon Tennis (10:44):** Orchestrates a full-stack, multi-device web app—streaming real-time mobile gyroscope sensor data over the network to control a 3D racket in a browser game while generating live play-by-play voice commentary using **Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS**.
