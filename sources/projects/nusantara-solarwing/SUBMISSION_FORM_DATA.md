Executive Summary / Abstract (212 words):

Indonesia's remote maritime sectors need repeatable surveillance, yet a small electric UAV can lose its return margin when clouds reduce solar input and headwinds raise return demand. Nusantara SolarWing proposes a solar-assisted fixed-wing aircraft and a wind-aware reserve planner for a conceptual patrol from Ranai, Natuna. The planner maximizes new search-line distance as a coverage proxy, subject to a protected battery reserve. At each waypoint it combines battery state, conservative solar input, and high-side return-energy estimates to command patrol, shorten, or return. A five-leg creeping-line pattern defines a 12 km by 8 km planning box with 2 km lane spacing, beginning 20 km from the Ranai reference point. A first-principles airframe model estimates 147.5 W level-flight draw and an 894 Wh nominal 6S12P battery. NASA POWER hourly irradiance near Natuna for September 2025 yields 29 of 30 days passing an eight-hour, no-wind energy screen at a 20% reserve. That count is not a route safety result. For a 20 km return, an assumed 5 m/s headwind raises modeled energy from 60.7 to 96.4 Wh. With the same pack, battery-only nominal endurance to reserve is 4.03 hours, versus up to eight modeled daylight hours with solar assistance. Bench, weather-replay, and supervised flight tests will verify power, forecast calibration, and coverage before operational use.
